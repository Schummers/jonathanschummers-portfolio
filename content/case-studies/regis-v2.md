## Context & Objectives

### Problem

A Luxembourg landlord owes the tax office one form 190 per property every year, has to justify every rent against a legal cap of 5% of the invested capital, and one day has to prove thirty years of improvement works at resale. All three obligations run on the same pile of invoices. In my family, that pile was a physical binder, one Excel per property, and a yearly scramble through bank statements and inboxes to find what was missing. The real challenge was not the tax rules. It was turning an annual panic into a weekly habit.

### Target audience

Luxembourg private landlords managing a few properties themselves, often 55 and over, who file at the real-cost regime and would rather photograph an invoice than type it.

### Team

- Me, product designer, PM and developer
- Cursor and Claude Code as the build team
- My parents, first users
- About twenty beta testers in Luxembourg, family and acquaintances

## How I solved this problem

### 1. Picked one legal obligation out of a thirty-year vision as the first brick

The long-term vision is a full rental management assistant: tax return, rent cap, resale gain, profitability, valuation, an AI agent on top. I did not start there. The yearly tax return is the only piece every landlord must do, every year, with a sanction behind it. Profitability and valuation are nice to have and not for everyone. And preparing the return forces the one thing every later feature needs: a clean, validated record of every invoice and transaction, accumulating year after year.
