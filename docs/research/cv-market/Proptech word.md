# How People Actually Talk About Real-Estate Startups: "Proptech" vs the Alternatives — and How a Senior Product Designer Should Frame His Real-Estate Angle

## TL;DR
- **"Proptech" is the dominant *insider* category label** — it is what VCs, accelerators, brokerages' innovation arms, conferences, and trade press use to group real-estate startups, and it is a legitimate, recognized tag in Crunchbase, CB Insights and PitchBook. But it is **niche jargon**: job-board listings mentioning "proptech" are outnumbered by "fintech" by roughly 15–30×, and the companies themselves rarely use the word on their own homepages.
- **The companies that *do* the work almost never call themselves "proptech."** Across a US sample (Zillow, Opendoor, Compass, Pacaso) and an EU sample (Masteos, Casavo, Homeloop, Bricksave), the self-description on homepages and About pages is overwhelmingly **"real estate technology company," "real estate platform," or "real estate marketplace."** "Proptech" appears mostly in third-party VC/press descriptions, and somewhat more readily on European companies than American ones.
- **Recommended framing for the CV/portfolio:** lead with **"real estate tech"** (plain, universally understood) and keep **"proptech"** as a secondary keyword for recruiter/VC search-matching — e.g. *"Senior Product Designer — 0→1 SaaS in fintech and real estate tech (proptech)."* This is widely understood in both the EU and US, signals the same insider fluency as "proptech" without alienating non-specialists, and lets his founder project + licensed background carry the credibility rather than a buzzword.

## Key Findings

### 1. The term inventory, ranked by real-world usage
From VC reports, company self-descriptions, press and job posts, the labels attach to real-estate startups in roughly this order of prevalence:

1. **"Proptech" / "PropTech" / "property technology"** — the umbrella category term of the *industry/investor* class. Used by Fifth Wall, MetaProp, Pi Labs, Concrete VC, JLL Spark, NAR's REACH, MIPIM PropTech, CRETI's annual VC report, and as a category hub on Crunchbase and an Expert Collection on CB Insights. Coined in Europe; per MetaProp's Aaron Block it had "a bit of a head start… it was known as proptech in Europe."
2. **"Real estate tech" / "real estate technology"** — the most common *plain-language* descriptor, used interchangeably with proptech by press (Crunchbase News: "real estate tech, or proptech, startups") and the preferred self-description on company homepages and in stock-market coverage ("real estate technology company" for Opendoor, Compass).
3. **"Real estate platform" / "real estate marketplace"** — the dominant *self-identification* for transaction/marketplace businesses (Casavo "residential marketplace," Pacaso "real estate marketplace," Masteos "real estate platform").
4. **"Real estate software"** — used for B2B SaaS tools (property management, CRM).
5. **"CRE tech" / "CREtech"** — commercial-real-estate-specific; a US-favored term. Per Built In/industry sources, in the US "the real estate tech space is also referred to as CRE tech."
6. **"Construction tech" / "contech"** — the construction-phase subset; a distinct, well-funded sub-category (contech led 2024 proptech VC with $4.5B per CRETI).
7. **"Real estate fintech" / "fintech for real estate"** — the lucrative overlap (mortgage, payments, fractional investing). Brendan Wallace, CEO/CIO of Fifth Wall, in Commercial Observer (Dec 2025): "The convergence of fintech and proptech has historically produced the biggest outcomes in proptech… you have two that stand out — Opendoor… and Bilt."
8. **"Housing tech," "rental tech," "built world / built environment technology"** — newer/niche. "Built world" is a deliberate rebrand by Fifth Wall and JLL Spark; "climate tech" increasingly overlaps the energy/ESG slice.

### 2. Volume, legitimacy and trend
- **Legitimacy: high among insiders, in both EU and US.** "Proptech" is a formal category in Crunchbase (a dedicated hub with ~1,880 for-profit companies tagged) and a CB Insights "Real Estate Tech" Expert Collection; it is used in the names of institutional programs (JLL Spark "Investors in PropTech," NAR's "PropTech Startup Showdown," MIPIM PropTech). Houlihan Lokey, EQT and CRETI publish "PropTech" reports drawing on PitchBook/CB Insights/S&P data. The word is fully legitimate as an *investment/industry* category.
- **Volume vs fintech: far smaller.** Job postings are the cleanest comparable metric: on Indeed (US, June 2026) fintech returned 9,556 postings vs 426 for proptech (~22×); on LinkedIn fintech postings outnumber proptech by roughly 14–30× in both the US and UK. VC funding: per Citi Ventures ("Why PropTech Is Poised for a Comeback"), "VC firms have poured almost $250 billion into the U.S. fintech ecosystem since 2013… Yet over the same period, proptechs have received less than one-fifth as much VC funding as fintechs, leading to the creation of less than one-fifth as many unicorns." Market size shows a similar gap: the global fintech market was valued at roughly $394.88B in 2025 (Fortune Business Insights) versus a global proptech market near $36.55B in 2024 (industry market data via Landbase) — an ~11× difference.
- **Trend direction:** the proptech *sector* peaked in VC funding in Q4 2021 and fell sharply afterward. It then rebounded — CRETI's annual report put 2024 proptech VC at $15.1B, a 32.5% YoY increase (contech leading at $4.5B, residential $3.6B, multifamily $2.95B). CRETI's 2025 analysis (via Multifamily Dive, Jan 2026) reported $16.7B invested globally in proptech and adjacent real-estate technology, a 67.9% YoY increase — a strong rebound that surpassed pre-pandemic 2019 levels. (Note: Crunchbase's own 2025 tally was more conservative, describing funding as only "slightly higher" than recent years with deal count at a multiyear low — the sources disagree on magnitude, agree on direction.) The *word* "proptech" is well established but has not gone mainstream the way "fintech" has.
- **"Proptech" is mostly insiders talking to insiders.** Jack Posthauer, U.S. VP at Probis (a Munich-based cost-controlling product for developers), told Commercial Observer (March 11, 2025) that "the proptech market is nowhere near what fintech is because it's just known within the real estate community… they much more commonly would say real estate tech, or just digitalization." In the same piece, Michael Beckerman, CEO of CREtech, said: "After nearly 40 years in commercial real estate — and a decade in CRE technology — I still don't know what 'proptech' really means. Most real estate companies don't either."
- **Verdict — EU:** "Proptech" is more widely recognized and self-applied in Europe (Casavo, Homeloop, Masteos all get tagged "proptech" in EU press; Pi Labs/Concrete VC brand around it). Safe to use, but pair with "real estate tech." **Verdict — US:** "Proptech" is insider/VC jargon; mainstream audiences and even many operators prefer "real estate technology." Use it as a secondary keyword, not the lead.

### 3. How companies self-identify (sample audit)
The pattern is consistent: **operators describe themselves by what they do in real estate, not as "proptech."**

**US sample (≈0/8 lead with "proptech" on their own homepage):**
- **Zillow** — "residential real estate," the "housing super app"; never "proptech" in its own shareholder letters/site.
- **Opendoor** — "real estate technology company" (Crunchbase/press); own site: "Real estate is broken. Come help us fix it."
- **Compass** — "a real estate technology company with a powerful end-to-end platform" (its own LinkedIn).
- **Pacaso** — "technology-enabled real estate marketplace" / "real estate company."
- **Roofstock, Doorvest, Belong Home, Latchel** — describe themselves around "real estate investing," "rental," "property management"; "proptech" appears only in third-party tags.

**EU sample (more "proptech" in press, but still rarely on homepages):**
- **Casavo** — "proptech scaleup" in VC/press; own positioning: "real estate technology company" / "residential marketplace."
- **Masteos** — "end-to-end real estate transaction and management platform"; homepage "we help you invest in rental housing"; "proptech" only in press.
- **Homeloop** — "proptech startup" in French press; own framing: "real estate transactions."
- **Bricksave / GetGround / Garantme / Hosman / Flatlooker / Manda** — self-describe as "investment platform," "real estate investment," "rental management," etc.; "proptech" is the journalist/VC label, not the homepage label.

**Self-identification rate for "proptech" as the primary homepage label: roughly 0–1 in 8 in the US, and a low minority in the EU** (more common in pitch/press contexts than on the marketing site).

### 4. How peers (designers/PMs in the sector) position themselves
- Real-world examples show a **combo vocabulary**, frequently pairing the sector with fintech and with plain "real estate." Example: a digital product designer's LinkedIn headline reads *"UX Designer | UX/UI, SaaS, FinTech & Proptech | Experience in Banking & Commercial Real Estate"* — note both "Proptech" *and* "commercial real estate" spelled out, plus "FinTech."
- Company-side job posts for the exact role use **"real estate technology"** as the framing (Compass: "real estate technology company"; Opendoor job tags: "Fintech • Real Estate • Software • PropTech" — i.e. they stack all the labels).
- Product/design talent inside these firms tends to describe the *domain* ("residential real estate," "home buying," "property management") plus the *model* (SaaS, marketplace, 0→1), rather than leaning on "proptech" alone.
- **Dominant peer vocabulary:** "real estate" / "real estate tech" as the anchor, with "proptech" and "fintech" added as adjacent keywords — exactly the stacked approach employers themselves use in job tags.

## Details
The historical record is consistent: "proptech" is a portmanteau (property + technology) modeled on "fintech," popularized in Europe in the 2010s and adopted globally as the investment-category label. Academic and industry histories (Oxford's Future of Real Estate Initiative using Crunchbase data; Andrew Baum's "PropTech 3.0") cemented it as the research/VC term. Its strength is precisely that it is a *category* word — useful for grouping companies, funds and conference tracks. Its weakness is that it remains an insider word: it carries weight with VCs, innovation teams and recruiters who specialize in the space, but it does not register with the general business audience the way "fintech" now does, and operators tend to avoid it in consumer-facing and even most B2B self-descriptions.

For a senior product designer, the practical implication is that "proptech" is a **keyword for discoverability** (recruiter and VC searches, ATS matching) rather than a **credibility signal** in itself. Credibility in this domain comes from demonstrated domain understanding — which, in this designer's case, is anchored by a licensed real-estate background and a founder project (an AI assistant for landlords), even though his shipped client work is in finance and heavy industry.

A note of honesty about his profile: his hands-on, shipped real-estate work is largely his own founder project, while his client track record is in fintech/industry. The framing should therefore **not overclaim** real-estate delivery. It should present real estate as a genuine, credible *angle* (founder + licensed background + an AI-for-landlords product) layered on top of a strong, verifiable 0→1 SaaS record in regulated/complex domains (bank onboarding, energy, heavy industry). That combination — regulated-industry SaaS chops + first-hand real-estate domain knowledge — is actually the exact profile real-estate-fintech teams say produces the biggest outcomes.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Pick the anchor label now.**
Use **"real estate tech"** as the primary label and **"proptech"** as a parenthetical/secondary keyword. It is understood by everyone, matches how operators and the press describe the space, and still captures insider search traffic. Keep "fintech" up front since that is where his shipped work actually is.

**Stage 2 — Put the keyword stack where search engines read it.**
On LinkedIn and the CV, mirror how employers tag these roles (e.g., Opendoor's own "Fintech • Real Estate • Software • PropTech"). Include "proptech" once in the LinkedIn headline or About section and once in the CV skills/keywords line so ATS and recruiter searches catch it — but don't make it the headline's first word.

**Stage 3 — Let the founder project + license carry the real-estate credibility.**
Name the AI-for-landlords product and the licensed background explicitly; that is what differentiates him from a generic SaaS designer claiming a real-estate angle.

**Benchmarks that would change this advice:**
- If he is **targeting EU proptech-specialist VCs/startups or accelerators (Pi Labs, Concrete VC, MIPIM PropTech)**, lead with "proptech" — that audience uses it as a badge of belonging.
- If he is **targeting US consumer real-estate brands (Zillow, Compass, Opendoor, Pacaso) or any non-specialist hiring manager**, lead with "real estate tech / real estate technology" and demote "proptech."
- If a **specific job description uses "proptech" repeatedly**, mirror it back verbatim in the application (ATS keyword matching).

**Candidate positioning sentences (CV / portfolio):**
1. *"Senior Product Designer with 6 years shipping 0→1 SaaS in regulated, complex domains — fintech, energy and heavy industry — now focused on real estate tech, where I'm building an AI assistant for landlords."*
2. *"I help fintech and real estate tech (proptech) teams turn complex, regulated workflows into products people actually want to use — from bank onboarding to tools for landlords."*
3. *"Product designer at the intersection of fintech and real estate: 0→1 SaaS for banks, energy and industry, plus a licensed real-estate background and a founder project building AI for landlords."*
4. *"0→1 SaaS product designer specializing in regulated industries (finance, energy, real estate). Licensed real-estate background + founder of an AI assistant for landlords — I understand the property domain from the inside."*
5. *"Senior Product Designer — fintech & real estate tech. Six years turning messy, high-stakes workflows into shipped SaaS; currently applying that to proptech via my own AI-for-landlords venture."*

## Caveats
- **Google Trends exact index values could not be verified.** Neither the main research nor the targeted follow-up could retrieve the precise 0–100 search-interest numbers (or clean US/EU segmentation) for proptech vs fintech vs real estate tech; the head-to-head ratio is therefore supported by *job-posting* counts, VC-funding ratios and market-size estimates rather than a verified Trends reading. The directional conclusion (fintech far larger; proptech smaller and post-2021-peak) is well supported; the precise multiplier on *search* volume is not.
- **Job-posting counts are platform-reported headers** that round and fluctuate; LinkedIn's own proptech-jobs page returned internally inconsistent counts. The ~15–30× fintech-to-proptech ratio is an order-of-magnitude finding, not a precise figure.
- **Sources disagree on 2025 funding magnitude.** CRETI reports a strong rebound (+67.9% YoY to $16.7B); Crunchbase characterizes 2025 as only marginally up with deal count at a multiyear low. They agree on the direction (recovery off the 2022–2023 trough) but not the size.
- **Company self-descriptions can change** and vary by page (homepage vs investor materials vs LinkedIn). Several EU companies are tagged "proptech" by VCs/press even when their own marketing says "real estate platform"; the audit reflects the dominant pattern, not every instance.
- **Some LinkedIn profile/headline evidence is illustrative**, drawn from publicly visible examples rather than a statistically representative sample; the "dominant vocabulary" conclusion is a qualitative read of how peers and employers phrase the sector.
- **Market-size figures are directional** and drawn from market-research vendors whose methodologies and category definitions differ; treat the ~11× fintech-vs-proptech gap as approximate.
